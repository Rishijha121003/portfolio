Anything less is A.
3. D names no default. Both D-pickers never answer what the recruiter sees in 20 seconds — D permits a second, beautifully coherent greeter. D's other sins: effort scored 2 for the most expensive option (corrected to 1); the Contrarian attacked "warm paper/serif as LLM-default cliché" then prescribed… a paper/ink/amber palette — taste-smuggling he correctly identified but then committed. His own D=32, E-caveat concession: "B only reaches D's ceiling by growing into D" — adopt D's ceiling inside B.
4. Alternatives inferior for named reasons:
   - A — preserves the verified defect: `/` SSRs LoginScreen; every route ships full OS via layout.tsx:97 with zero next/dynamic; metadata advertises the gimmick while crawlers see greeter HTML. "Do more of the thing that is already failing, prettier." Rejected.
   - C — severs resolveRouteLaunch + single PROJECT_RECORDS feed; duplicates content for a solo student; splits SEO equity; kills the "one honest story" that makes the OS a legible exhibit. 48 presentation files do not justify standalone deployment. Rejected.
   - D — same architecture debt as A (no lazy boundary named), maximum cost, no default, two coordinated design systems for one maintainer. Its one real idea (differentiated-but-shared identity tokens) is absorbed into B. Rejected as option; retained as execution detail.

Why B is the right shape, independent of matrices: backend hiring is evidence-dense and time-poor. The 20-second artifact is: name, role, two repo-linked backend projects above the fold, OS as memorable second act for the 3-in-10 who click deeper. B delivers exactly that; D delays it; A never delivers it; C hides it behind a URL nobody pastes into a recruiter's inbox.
